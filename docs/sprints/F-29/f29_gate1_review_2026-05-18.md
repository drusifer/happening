## Notes

### Note 1 — AC-F29-2-1: Sunrise icon position vs. gradient start (Non-blocking)

**HEURISTIC:** #2 Match Between System and Real World
**SURFACE:** Solar icon placement
**ISSUE:** AC-F29-2-1 places the sunrise icon at "civil twilight begin (start of dawn)" — roughly 30 minutes *before* the sun actually rises. A sun icon at a pre-sunrise position violates the user's mental model: if they see a sun icon, they expect it to mark the moment the sun appears above the horizon.

**FIX:** Split the two concerns cleanly:
- Gradient *starts* at civil twilight begin (correct — dawn glow begins here)
- Sunrise icon appears at actual **sunrise** time
- Sunset icon appears at actual **sunset** time
- Gradient *ends* at civil twilight end

The civil twilight boundaries drive the gradient; the actual sun events drive the icons.

---

### Note 2 — AC-F29-1-3: Decimal lat/lng is a high cognitive barrier (Non-blocking)

**HEURISTIC:** #6 Recognition Rather Than Recall
**SURFACE:** Manual location entry
**ISSUE:** Asking users to type `40.7143` and `-74.0060` is developer-level input. Most users have no idea what their coordinates are. The geolocator button covers the majority case (device location), but the fallback leaves users stranded if they deny permission.

**FIX (recommended):** Add a city/region name search field as the primary manual fallback. Resolve the city to coordinates client-side (small bundled lookup table or a one-time OS geocode call). Show "New York, NY → 40.7143, -74.006" as confirmation. Raw coordinate entry can remain as an advanced/override option below the search field.

This doesn't block the sprint — the geolocator path covers most users — but it should be in scope before shipping.

---

### Note 3 — AC-F29-3-3: Rising vs. setting moon icon distinction at strip height (Non-blocking)

**HEURISTIC:** #8 Aesthetic and Minimalist Design
**SURFACE:** Moonset icon style
**ISSUE:** "Same phase, dimmer or outlined style to indicate setting" is a subtle visual distinction at ~28–36px. At strip height, the difference between a filled waxing-crescent and a dimmed waxing-crescent is likely invisible — especially for users without perfect vision or on lower-DPI displays.

**FIX:** Consider a directional cue instead of or in addition to dimming: a small downward arrow below the moon icon for moonset, upward arrow for moonrise. This maps to the physical metaphor (rising vs. setting) without relying on opacity differences.

Morpheus to decide during design; this is a concern to evaluate during the icon design phase, not a blocker.

---

### Note 4 — AC-F29-3-5: Moon badge placement near settings gear (Non-blocking)

**HEURISTIC:** #3 User Control and Freedom
**SURFACE:** Static moon phase badge location
**ISSUE:** Placing a decorative badge "near the settings gear" risks reducing the tap/click target size for the settings button — a critical control. On a ~28–36px strip, every pixel counts.

**FIX:** The badge should sit *left* of the settings gear with a minimum 8px separation, and must not reduce the gear's interactive target area. Morpheus to specify exact layout in architecture.

---

## Summary

All four user stories have clear, testable acceptance criteria. The feature is well-scoped, fully offline, and the geolocator + Dart astronomy library approach is clean. The notes above are design refinements, not story-level problems.

**Gate 1: APPROVED** — proceed to Morpheus architecture.
Notes 1 and 2 are the most important; please ensure they're addressed in AC or arch before implementation begins.
